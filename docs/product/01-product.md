# Product

## In a nutshell

A motion video service for social media. A person writes what they need or drops a link, gets three ready variants in the style of their brand, and edits the one they like with words: "bigger headline", "faster", "make it vertical". There is a manual editor, but it is hidden.

Positioning in one phrase: **"Claude Code, but for videos"**. You say what you need and edit with words, not sliders.

## Where the idea came from

- The author is a programmer. He tried Higgsfield and got confused: the service is "about everything and nothing", and the interface demands figuring it out, although the user is technically savvy.
- He noticed that a combination of Claude, HyperFrames, Whisper and ffmpeg (including free tools) can assemble motion videos with graphics without much trouble.
- Conclusion: an ordinary marketer has no convenient tool for motion graphics.

This is the history of the idea, not a choice of technologies. What to build the product with has not been decided yet.

## Who it is for

**Main audience:** marketers and social media managers in small companies, freelancers (including designers who do not do motion), small agencies.
- They need 5–20 videos a month.
- They do not know After Effects and do not want to learn it.
- Hiring a motion designer is expensive.

**Deliberately not for:** the "casual user" who needs a video once a year. They pay poorly and leave quickly. This audience was dropped at the start of the discussion.

## What the product promises

1. Describe the video in words or give a link.
2. Get three variants with your brand's colors, fonts and logo.
3. Click any spot in the frame and say what to change.
4. Download in the format you need for social media.

## What makes it different

- **Simplicity.** One field. No choosing models, modes or credits, the things that confuse people in Higgsfield.
- **Editing with words and clicks.** Everything the service can do can be requested in words, even if there is no button.
- **Brand once.** All videos are in the company's style from the start.
- **Hard to make it bad.** Sizes, margins, timing and colors are held by the system, so even random actions give a decent result.
- **Motion graphics, not generated footage.** Text, numbers, interfaces, shapes: crisp text and exact brand colors, not AI-generated people and scenes.
- **A manual editor for the meticulous**, but it is hidden and limited to the order, length and text of scenes.

## Product principles

1. **Everything is available with words.** The interface is bare but does not restrict. We add buttons only for what people ask for most often.
2. **Start from what the person has:** an idea, a text or a link, an existing video, a podcast recording.
3. **The service proposes, the person agrees.** Especially for an existing video: the service places the spots for graphics itself.
4. **Choose with the eyes, not by configuring.** Three variants instead of parameters.
5. **No dead ends.** After each step there are two or three next ones.
6. **Hard to make it bad.**
7. **The interface teaches along the way.** A meticulous user finds the manual editor on their own, and it does not get in the way of the rest.

The main gesture, which must be memorable and look good in a demo: **click anywhere in the frame and say what to change.**

## Risks

- **No protection from copying.** What is built from open tools can be built by others too.
- **Distribution.** There are competitors, but few people know about them. So the main problem of the market is getting the product to people, and we will have it too.
- **Quality the first time.** The user cannot fix a video by hand, so the main work is templates, animation and taste, not the interface.
- **Economics.** Every edit costs money, and the user can redo a video dozens of times.

## How we will know it works

- How many people make a second video (retention).
- How many free users switch to Pro.
- How many edits it takes before download.
- How much one video costs including all edits.
- Revenue growth from month to month.
